What Makes Something Funny?

To understand why certain lines can make people laugh, we first have to understand the theories behind humor.

Theories of Comedy

  1. Relief Theory
    • Buildup of tension is inherent to all humorous scenarios- the perception of humor is directly related to the release of that tension.
    • EX: “The worst is when you ask someone on a date and they turn you down. ‘Cause what they’re really saying is, ‘you know what? I don’t even feel like eating a free meal around you” (Jim Gaffigan).

  2. Superiority Theory
    • Humor is derived from the misfortunes of others and our own relative superiority. The joke not only lies in the superiority, but in the creative and interesting ways people insult each other. “Yo momma is overweight” is not nearly as funny as “Yo momma is so fat she walked in front of the TV and I missed three seasons.”
    • EX: "Yo momma..."

  3. Incongruity Theory
    • Fundamentally incompatible concepts or unexpected resolutions are funny.
    • EX: “I am a man of my word. And that word is unreliable” (Demitri Martin).

  4. Benign Violations Theory
    • The following three conditions are necessary:
      1. The presence of a norm violation
        • IE: Robbing a retirement home, breaking up via text, purposefully sneezing on a child
      2. A “benign” or “safe” context in which the violation takes place.
      3. The interpretation of the first two points simultaneously. One must interpret a violation as relatively harmless.

From here, we can figure out how individual jokes are written, commonly via the Comedic Forumla.

The Comedic Formula

  1. Extablish Expectations (Set Up)
  2. Build Anticipation (Rising Action)
  3. Defy Expectations (Punchline)

Example: “My wife and I were happy for ten years,” [sets expectation- a marriage] "really, life was good," [builds anticipation] “and then we met.” [Subverts expectation, emotionally triggers laugh]
